# Mobile homes and modular houses

These are two different things that get marketed as one, and the confusion costs
buyers real money. Be direct with the user here — this is the area where the gap
between what is advertised and what is legal is widest.

## Mobile homes (casas móveis, tiny houses, houses on wheels)

**The claim:** put one on cheap rustic land, no licence needed, because it is
"mobile" and therefore not a building.

**The reality:** despite the name, mobile homes cannot be built, installed or
permanently placed on any land without municipal licensing. The only exception is
a camping park or caravan site operating under its own previously approved
licence.

Installing without permits is illegal and exposes the owner to municipal fines
and to forced demolition and removal. The buyer loses the unit and the money spent
siting it.

The confusion is actively fuelled by companies selling the units and by agents
selling rustic land with the promise attached. When a listing says a plot is
"ideal for a tiny house", treat it as a claim to verify at the Câmara, not as a
feature — and record it as a red flag on the listing.

**Before buying land for this purpose:**
- Ask the Câmara Municipal directly about zoning and licensing for that plot.
- If the goal is to run a camping or caravan park, confirm the land already holds
  the required licence or can obtain it — that is a business licensing process,
  not a housing one.
- Consider a prefabricated or modular home that can be properly licensed as a
  permanent building instead.
- Be sceptical of any seller who says no permit is needed. Consult a lawyer or
  architect before committing money.

## Modular homes (casas modulares)

Factory-built structures assembled on site — increasingly popular and entirely
legitimate. Two common types: **wooden** and **LSF (light steel frame)**. Designs
range from minimal studios to architect-designed villas.

**The key difference from mobile homes:** modular houses are intended as permanent
structures and must comply with construction law. Properly licensed, they can be
installed on urban or urbanizável land as a primary residence, holiday home or
tourism accommodation.

### Price and quality
Pricing is wide — from very cheap models to premium solutions exceeding
traditional construction costs. The real advantage is **speed**: once permits are
secured, build and assembly can take weeks to a few months.

Visit the showroom or completed units, especially for very low-priced offers.
Build quality, insulation and finishes are where cheap models economise, and
insulation in particular is not something you can retrofit cheaply.

### Licensing
Exactly like any traditional home: architectural plans, engineering validation,
and project approval by the Câmara Municipal. Modular construction is not a
shortcut around licensing — it is a shortcut through the build phase only.

**Before buying land for a modular home:**
- Ensure the land is urban (or urbanizável with a realistic path).
- Confirm the supplier provides project files compatible with municipal approval —
  many do not, and the buyer discovers it after paying a deposit.
- Involve an architect or legal adviser early.
- Check the team can coordinate the structural project, which is where
  responsibility often falls between the supplier and the local architect.

### Choosing a supplier
Compare materials, warranty and after-sales support, not only price. Research the
builder's completed projects, and visit a finished house rather than a showroom
model where possible.

## Red flags

- Any listing or supplier promising "no permit needed"
- Rustic land marketed as suitable for a mobile home outside a licensed park
- A modular supplier who cannot supply municipality-ready project documentation
- Prices that undercut the market substantially with no explanation of the spec
