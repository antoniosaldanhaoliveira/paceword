# Urban land (terreno urbano)

Land inside a designated urban area on the municipal plan, served by
infrastructure (water, electricity, sewage, road), and earmarked for urban uses.
It is the most direct type to build on, and it prices accordingly.

## The valuation factors

Work through these in order; the first two dominate everything else.

**1. Building index and planning rules.** What may actually be built:
implantação (footprint), área bruta de construção (total floor area), number of
floors, basement potential. Also the obligations — mandatory setbacks, cedências
for road widening, parking requirements. A plot is worth what you can build on it,
so a large cheap plot with a 15% index can be worth less than a small dear one
with a 40% index.

**2. Location.** Central and serviced, or isolated? Established neighbourhood?
Access to shops, schools, transport?

**3. Sun orientation.** South or west-facing is materially more valuable, and
permanently so — no renovation fixes a north-facing plot.

**4. Views.** Sea or open panorama commands a real premium, and is rare.

**5. Privacy and surroundings.** Overlooked by neighbours? Quality of the
surrounding architecture? Noise sources — main roads, industry, agricultural
machinery, church bells, dogs?

**6. Topography.** Slope raises construction cost sharply — retaining walls,
excavation, access ramps — while sometimes buying a view. Assume a steep plot is
the expensive option until an engineer says otherwise.

**7. Access.** Paved road to the boundary? Is vehicle access practical for
construction machinery, not just for a car?

**8. Width and shape.** A narrow or awkward plot constrains the layout before the
architect starts. A 500 m² square and a 500 m² ribbon are not the same asset.

**9. Infrastructure.** Urban plots usually have water, electricity and sewage at
the boundary — confirm it rather than assume, and confirm the distance and the
connection cost.

**10. Trees and natural features.** Adds perceived value; check for protected
species (notably cork oaks and holm oaks, whose felling is restricted).

**11. Cleanliness.** A cleared plot shows its potential and sells faster.

**12. External constraints.** High-voltage lines, watercourses, gas easements,
antennae, rights of way.

**The judgement:** a plot does not have to be perfect, but the positives must
clearly outweigh the negatives. One severe negative — north-facing, no legal
access, a line crossing the middle — becomes the obstacle at resale even if the
buyer can live with it.

## Legal analysis

**Confirm the classification.** Urban (build now), *urbanizável* (may become
urban at a future PDM revision), or rustic. Urbanizável is not worthless, but
construction depends on a zoning revision that may take years and may never
happen — price it as an option, not as a building plot.

**Confirm the indexes in writing**, from the PDM or a certidão, not from the
agent.

**Check for watercourses.** A line crossing the land typically requires a 10 m
buffer, or 5 m with APA (Agência Portuguesa do Ambiente) consultation. On a
narrow plot this can eliminate the buildable area entirely.

**Check the cedências.** Road-widening obligations can shave metres off the front
of the plot and change what fits.

## The two kinds of urban plot

**Standard urban plots** — flexible. Potentially one house, several, a housing
project, division into smaller plots, or a formal loteamento. Each still depends
on local rules, but the freedom is real and suits investors who want to maximise
the land.

**Plots within a loteamento (approved subdivision)** — predefined rules on height,
volume and design, usually one single-family house per lot, and infrastructure
already at the entrance. Two extra documents are mandatory here: the **alvará de
loteamento** and the **planta síntese**.

For a buyer in a hurry, a loteamento plot is usually the better choice. Much of
the approval burden is already discharged, the licensing path is shorter, and the
infrastructure cost — which is easy to underestimate badly on a raw plot — is
already spent.

## Red flags

- Advertised as "urban" but the caderneta says rústico and the PDM has not been
  revised
- No construction index quoted anywhere, and the agent estimates one verbally
- A subdivision with no alvará — illegal loteamentos exist and the Câmara is the
  only authority that settles it
- Access across a neighbour's land with no registered right of way
- Infrastructure "nearby" without a stated distance or connection quote
