# Houses, apartments and quintas

Ready-to-use property, which makes it feel simpler than land. It is not — the
risks just move from "can I build?" to "what am I inheriting?"

Typical categories: city and town apartments; detached or semi-detached villas;
old village houses needing renovation; quintas and farmhouses in rural areas.

## Why buy here

Speed of occupation, no construction or licensing to manage, faster income if
renting, and — for a renovation — a controlled budget *only if* the project is
properly costed first.

## What to analyse

### 1. Legal status
- Is there a **Licença de Utilização**? (Pre-1951 buildings are exempt but need a
  certidão confirming the construction date.)
- Is it correctly registered in **both** the land registry and the tax office,
  with matching descriptions and areas?
- Is there any construction or extension not reflected in the official documents?
  Unlicensed additions are widespread. They resurface at resale, at mortgage
  approval, and at AL licensing — and legalising one after the fact can cost more
  than it added.
- **For apartments:** is the condominium organised and financially sound? Read the
  minutes (actas) and accounts. Approved-but-unbilled works are a real liability
  that transfers with the flat.

### 2. Structural condition
- Roof, plumbing, electrical installation — the three expensive systems.
- Damp, cracks, termites. Termites are common in older timber-framed houses in the
  centre and north and are not a cosmetic problem.
- Genuinely renovated, or cosmetically refreshed over unresolved defects?

**Commission a pre-purchase technical inspection.** This is the guide's first tip
and it earns its cost twice: once by telling you what you are buying, and again as
documented leverage in the negotiation. Insist on it for anything old or rural.

### 3. Location and surroundings
Urban or rural, quiet or busy street, proximity to schools, shops and transport.
For countryside houses, verify infrastructure specifically: electricity capacity,
water source and its licence, internet (fibre coverage is patchy and matters if
anyone works from home), and road maintenance responsibility in winter.

### 4. Future potential
Can it be extended or altered? What do the local rules allow? Would it work for
**AL (Alojamento Local)** or long-term rental? AL registration is restricted or
suspended in parts of some municipalities — confirm for the specific parish
rather than the city, since the rules are geographically fine-grained and change.

## For a renovation

- Get a contractor estimate — and be honest that a reliable one is very hard to
  obtain without a project. Early figures are indicative at best.
- Confirm the Câmara will approve the changes you intend before you commit.
- Check structural integrity, especially in old stone buildings where a wall may
  be two skins of rubble with no tie.
- Compute the real total: price + taxes + fees + works + contingency. A renovation
  that reaches the cost of new construction is common in old rural houses, and it
  is better to know that before the deed than after.
- Get quotes from several contractors — a 30% spread on identical work is normal.
- Sign a fixed-price contract with a timeline and delay penalties.

## Documents to request

Caderneta Predial · Certidão Permanente do Registo Predial · Licença de
Utilização · Certificado Energético · Ficha Técnica de Habitação (post-2004) ·
project plans and blueprints if renovating · condominium minutes and accounts for
apartments. Details in `documents.md`.

## When this is the right choice

When the buyer wants to avoid licensing and construction, needs somewhere livable
or rentable soon, wants passive income or a lifestyle change quickly, has a fixed
budget with little tolerance for variation, and does not want to absorb the
surprises that old buildings produce.
