# Document checklist

## Always, for every property type

### Caderneta Predial (Urbana or Rústica)
**Proves:** the fiscal registration — description, areas, location, patrimonial
value (VPT), current registered taxpayer.
**Source:** Portal das Finanças (owner can print it in a minute; a copy costs
nothing), or the local Serviço de Finanças.
**Look for:** the *article* type (urbano vs rústico), the recorded areas, whether
any building is registered, and whether the description matches what you saw.
The classification here is fiscal — it does **not** decide whether you can build.

### Certidão Permanente do Registo Predial
**Proves:** legal ownership and the full chain of charges — mortgages, seizures,
easements, usufructs, pre-emption rights.
**Source:** predialonline.pt (an access code valid for 6 months, ~€15) or the
Conservatória do Registo Predial.
**Look for:** the seller's name matching the registered owner; open mortgages
(they must be cancelled at the deed); penhoras; rights of way in favour of
neighbours; any *ónus* clause. Also check that the described property matches the
caderneta — divergences between the two registries are common and take months to
rectify.

## Land, additionally

### PDM classification / Planta de Localização e Condicionantes
**Proves:** what the municipal plan says about this exact plot, and which overlays
apply (RAN, REN, Natura 2000, coastal, forest, heritage).
**Source:** the Câmara Municipal's online GIS where it exists, otherwise the
Urbanismo counter. Some municipalities issue a formal *certidão de teor de PDM*.
**Look for:** the zone class and its permitted uses, the construction indexes,
and every overlay. This is the document that actually answers "can I build here".

### Certidão de Viabilidade / PIP (Pedido de Informação Prévia)
**Proves:** a binding municipal answer to a specific proposal — this many square
metres, this use, this footprint.
**Source:** submitted to the Câmara by an architect; takes weeks to months.
**When:** essential for ruins, rustic land and tourism projects. The guide's
recommendation is to buy only with an approved PIP, or with a CPCV conditional on
one. Counter advice at the Câmara is orientation, not a commitment.

### For a plot inside a loteamento
- **Alvará de Loteamento** — proves the subdivision was legally approved, and
  lists the building rules that bind each lot.
- **Planta Síntese** — the master plan showing each lot and what is permitted.
Without an alvará, be direct: an unlicensed subdivision is a known failure mode,
and the Câmara is the only source that settles it.

## Houses and apartments, additionally

### Licença de Utilização (habitation licence)
**Proves:** the building is legally authorised for habitation.
**Source:** the Câmara Municipal. Buildings from before 7 August 1951 are exempt
and instead need a certidão confirming the construction date.
**Look for:** the licensed use matching the actual use, and the licensed layout
matching the built layout. Unlicensed extensions are extremely common and become
the buyer's problem at resale, at mortgage, and at rental licensing.

### Certificado Energético
Legally required for a sale to complete, and a genuine indicator of what heating
will cost in a stone house with no insulation.

### Ficha Técnica de Habitação
For buildings constructed or refurbished after 2004 — the materials and technical
specification.

### For apartments
- **Condominium accounts and minutes (actas)** — arrears, disputes, and any
  approved-but-unbilled works. An approved roof replacement can arrive as a
  five-figure bill weeks after the deed.
- The condominium's declaration that the seller's quotas are paid.

### If renovating
Project plans and blueprints approved by the Câmara — without them you are
budgeting a renovation blind, which the guide flags as the standard way costs run
away.

## For a ruin, additionally

- **Municipal certification that the ruin is a legally recognised building**, and
  a statement of what may be rebuilt. Some municipalities refuse to recognise a
  structure that has fully collapsed even when it remains in the documents.
- Old blueprints, licences, or historical aerial imagery proving the building
  existed — useful evidence when the registry is thin.
- An approved PIP before completing. This is the single most valuable protection
  in a ruin purchase.

## Personal documents the buyer needs

- **NIF** (Portuguese tax number) — mandatory for any transaction. Available to
  non-residents; a fiscal representative is required for non-EU residents.
- **Portuguese bank account** — for IMT, stamp duty, utilities and the deed.
- **Passport or national ID**, plus proof of address (home country is fine).

Anyone may buy property in Portugal — Portuguese, EU/EEA, or non-EU, resident or
not. There is no nationality restriction on ownership. Residency is a separate
question from buying, and buying does not by itself grant it.

## Practical ordering

Ask for caderneta and certidão at first contact, before any visit is worth
arranging. Request the PDM classification before making an offer. Get the PIP
before completing on anything where buildability is the reason for the purchase.
