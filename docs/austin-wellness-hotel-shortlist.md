Two readings. **Smaller parcels carry a per-acre premium**, so acreage gets
cheaper per unit as you go up — which works in your favour given the
impervious-cover math pushes you larger. And **West Lake Hills trades at roughly
2.5× the Lakeway rate**, for land you most likely cannot build this on. You are
paying a premium for an address that will not permit the use.

*These are asking prices from search indexes, in a non-disclosure state. They are
not transaction evidence. See the guide, §4.*

## The structured-parking decision — resolved

I said this trade should be run before shortlisting. Here it is, at 145 spaces
(midpoint of the 120–170 the programme needs).

**2026 cost per stall:** surface $3,000–8,000 · above-grade deck $18,000–35,000 ·
underground $60,000–120,000.

| Option | Extra build cost | Land saved @15% (BSZ) | Value of land saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Above-grade deck** | $1.45M – $4.64M | 5.0 – 6.6 acres | **$2.49M – $8.28M** |
| Underground | $7.54M – $16.97M | 5.0 – 6.6 acres | $2.49M – $8.28M |

**Build the deck.** In the Barton Springs Zone an above-grade structure pays for
itself on land savings alone, and comfortably so at West Austin land values —
before counting the site-plan freedom it buys you.

**Do not go underground for land economics.** It costs two to three times what
it saves. Underground is justified only by aesthetics, topography, or a site so
constrained there is no alternative — all legitimate reasons for a $500 ADR
property, but they are design arguments, not land arguments.

Second-order effect worth modelling: a deck cuts the parking footprint out of
your impervious budget, which frees cover for **pool decks, terraces and hard
landscape** — the outdoor activation that is the product. On a 15%-cover site
that reallocation may matter more than the acreage.

## Next actions, in order

### A. The § 25-8-26 inquiry — do this first, it is free

The entire grandfathered-cover strategy rests on one unconfirmed point. Send
this to City of Austin Development Services and Watershed Protection:

> **Subject:** Redevelopment exception under § 25-8-26 — change of use to hotel
>
> I am evaluating existing commercial and office properties in the Barton
> Springs Zone for redevelopment as a hotel with associated spa and food and
> beverage.
>
> 1. Does the redevelopment exception under City Code § 25-8-26 apply where the
>    redevelopment involves a **change of use** — for example from office to
>    hotel — provided total impervious cover on the site is not increased?
> 2. If it does, what documentation establishes the site's existing impervious
>    cover for this purpose, and is a current survey sufficient?
> 3. Are there additional water-quality or treatment requirements triggered by
>    the change of use, even where cover does not increase?
> 4. Does the answer differ between the recharge zone, the Barton Creek
>    watershed, and the contributing zone?
>
> I am not asking for a determination on a specific parcel at this stage.

**If the answer is no, Priority 1 collapses and the search moves to the Lake
Austin watershed and Water Supply zones.** Ask before you spend anything else.

### B. Watershed determination on the shortlist

For each surviving candidate, pull the City of Austin property profile and
TCAD record and establish: watershed and zone, current zoning, city limits vs
ETJ vs county, existing impervious cover, floodplain (both FEMA and Austin's
Atlas 14 mapping), and Critical Water Quality Zone.

Do this before touring. It will remove candidates faster than any site visit.

### C. Broker brief — send verbatim

Named starting points: **Marcus & Millichap Austin** (9600 N MoPac Expy; active
hospitality division with Austin hotel transaction history), plus the Austin
offices of **JLL Hotels & Hospitality** and **CBRE Hotels**. Add one West Austin
land specialist.

> **Requirement — West Austin hotel/spa site**
>
> Boutique membership hotel-spa, approximately 50 keys, positioned at $500+ ADR,
> with significant outdoor amenity. Within 25 minutes of downtown Austin.
>
> **Acquiring either:** an existing hotel, office or industrial building for
> conversion, **or** land. Existing buildings are preferred — see below.
>
> **The binding constraint is impervious cover, not price.** In the Barton
> Springs Zone at 15% cover, the programme needs 11–17 acres with surface
> parking, or 6–10 acres with a parking deck. Accordingly:
>
> - Existing commercial or office buildings with **high grandfathered impervious
>   cover** are of most interest, and we will look at 3+ acres on that basis.
> - Raw land in the BSZ recharge zone is only of interest at **10+ acres**.
> - **Water Supply zones (Lake Austin watershed) at 20–40% cover are of strong
>   interest** at 4+ acres.
>
> **Must have:** hotel use permitted or a credible path to it; confirmed
> wastewater capacity; usable outdoor area outside floodplain and Critical Water
> Quality Zone.
>
> **Not interested in:** anything inside West Lake Hills or Rollingwood city
> limits; condominium interests; sites with no wastewater path.
>
> Off-market strongly preferred. Please include watershed, existing impervious
> cover and wastewater arrangement in any submission.

That last paragraph does real work. It tells a broker within thirty seconds
that you understand the market, which changes what they send you.

### D. Set the budget

The $2–20M range in the profile is a placeholder I chose, not a number you gave
me. The indexed comps put West Austin commercial land at **$500k–1.26M/acre**,
so a 10-acre BSZ site is $5–12M of land before any construction. HVS publishes
an annual US hotel development cost survey worth pulling for the vertical side.

**Give me the capital stack and I will rebuild the search bands around it** —
the difference between a $6M and a $15M site budget changes which of the four
target zones are even in play.

## What I could not do, and what it would take

| Blocked | Why | How to unblock |
|---|---|---|
| Verified live listings with price, acreage, status | LoopNet/Crexi/Realtor return 403 to automated access | A logged-in session, or a broker running the search |
| Per-parcel watershed and impervious cover | Requires the City of Austin property profile per parcel | Doable manually; ~15 min per candidate |
| Sold comparables | Texas is a non-disclosure state | An agent with MLS access, or CoStar |
| Off-market inventory | Not published anywhere | Brokers. At this size, two or three of them know every candidate |

The honest position: **at 5–20 acres, hotel-capable, West Austin, the qualifying
inventory is small enough that a good broker knows all of it.** The screening
work above is what makes you worth their time — but it does not substitute for
them.
