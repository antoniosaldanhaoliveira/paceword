### 4. 108 S Ranch Road 620, Lakeway 78734 — 9.44 acres, $4,700,000

**On the regulatory math this is the strongest site on the list. On character
and distance it is the weakest. Both statements are true and the tension is the
whole decision.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Site | **9.44 acres** (some listings say 9.47) |
| Price | **$4,700,000** — ~$498,000/acre, **$11.43/ft²** |
| Location | SW corner of RM 620 and Clara Van, **opposite the Lakeway Lexus dealership** |
| Jurisdiction | **City of Lakeway** — its own municipality, not Austin, not Austin ETJ |
| Available | Site-approved plan and topographical survey; listed via Horizon Realty |
| On market | LoopNet entries from **May 2024 and October 2024** — roughly **1.5–2 years** |

#### The cover math is not close

Lakeway sets its own impervious cover limits, and they bear no relation to
Austin's watershed regime:

- **60%** of net site area for development other than single-family/duplex
- **50%** single-family/duplex
- **40%** in certain districts
- Transfer of impervious cover credit is possible at the city's discretion
  (Code art. 28.10)

| Cap | Allowed cover | vs full programme | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lakeway, 60%** | **246,724 ft²** | **2.24×** | passes with room to spare |
| Lakeway, 40% | 164,483 ft² | 1.49× | still passes |
| *Barton Springs Zone, 15%* | *61,681 ft²* | *0.56×* | *would fail outright* |

**At 60% the programme uses 110,300 ft² and leaves roughly 136,000 ft² — about
3.1 acres — for pool decks, terraces and hard landscape.** The outdoor
activation that is the actual product stops being a constraint and becomes a
design opportunity. Nothing in the Barton Springs Zone offers that at this
acreage.

#### Zoning is a real path, not a fight

Lakeway has a **Hotel/Motel (HM) district**, written for "hotels, motels, motor
courts, lodges, and inns to provide accommodations for visitors to the city."
Hotels are contemplated in the code — the opposite of West Lake Hills.

The posture is favourable too: in **April and May 2026 the City Council moved to
amend its special-use and conditional-use permitting** specifically to make it
"more practical and consistent with the city's development needs." A city
loosening its permitting is a very different counterparty from one defending
its residential character.

Two things to establish: the parcel's **current zoning** and whether the path is
rezoning to HM, a **PUD**, or a special use permit; and whether any part of the
site sits **within 300 feet of residential or school property**, which triggers
a discretionary SUP with hearings before both the Zoning and Planning Commission
and Council.

#### Utilities — a genuine advantage

**Lakeway MUD supplies both water and wastewater**, serving 4,200 water and
3,000 wastewater customers, and is actively expanding (S-5 water recycling plant
expansion, a new elevated storage tank). For a spa — water-intensive, effluent-
heavy — a piped utility rather than a commercial OSSF removes the single
largest technical risk on any Hill Country site.

Get a **written service availability letter for this parcel**, and ask about the
commercial density multiplier: restaurants are billed at 2× the volume rate, and
a spa with food and beverage will be assessed on the same logic.

#### The two real problems

**1. Distance. It breaks your stated criterion.** Lakeway is ~25 miles
north-west of downtown. Realistic drive times are **30–40 minutes normally and
around 45 at peak** via RM 620 — and this site is *on* RM 620, not on the faster
Highway 71. Only an off-peak run down 71 approaches 25 minutes, and that is not
this parcel's route.

**2. Character. A wellness sanctuary opposite a Lexus dealership.** The site
fronts a commercial arterial in a car-dealership and retail context. At $500+
ADR you are selling seclusion and landscape, and the approach sequence is part
of the product. Nine and a half acres is enough to build inward and turn away
from the road — and the fact that a topographical survey is offered suggests
useful grade change — but this needs to be seen on the ground before anything
else. **It is the first thing to go and look at.**

#### The question this raises about your brief

Your 25-minute rule may be borrowed from urban hotel logic and may not serve
this product. Austin's actual $500+ ADR wellness comp set is **not** downtown-
proximate: Miraval Austin sits on 220 acres out by Lake Travis, and Lake Austin
Spa Resort is out the same way. Destination wellness in this market has always
been further out, and guests accept the drive as part of the arrival.

The membership side cuts the other way — members come weekly, guests come once,
so proximity matters far more for them. But it may not need to be proximity *to
downtown*. Lakeway, Bee Cave and Steiner Ranch are among the most affluent
catchments in the metro, with medians above $550,000 and Lakeway itself around
$572,500. **A Lakeway membership base would be local to Lakeway** rather than
commuting from the city.

That is a strategic choice, not a detail: an urban-adjacent club with a hotel
attached, or a destination resort with a local membership. This site only works
if the answer is the second. **Worth deciding deliberately rather than by
default.**

#### What to verify, in order

1. **Walk it.** Character and grade decide whether the rest is worth pursuing.
2. **Current zoning** and the entitlement path — HM rezoning, PUD or SUP.
3. **The 300-foot residential/school adjacency** test.
4. Lakeway MUD **written service availability** for this parcel, plus the
   commercial density multiplier.
5. What "site-approved plan" actually means — an approved site plan for a
   different use may or may not carry any value to you.
6. **Why it has sat for 18–24 months.** Long marketing on commercial land is
   either a price problem or a site problem, and the answer is negotiating
   leverage either way.

## The structured-parking decision — resolved

I said this trade should be run before shortlisting. Here it is, at 145 spaces
(midpoint of the 120–170 the programme needs).

**2026 cost per stall:** surface $3,000–8,000 · above-grade deck $18,000–35,000 ·
underground $60,000–120,000.

| Option | Extra build cost | Land saved @15% (BSZ) | Value of land saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Above-grade deck** | $1.45M – $4.64M | 5.0 – 6.6 acres | **$2.49M – $8.28M** |
| Underground | $7.54M – $16.97M | 5.0 – 6.6 acres | $2.49M – $8.28M |

**Build the deck.** In the Barton Springs Zone an above-grade structure pays for
itself on land savings alone, and comfortably so at West Austin land values —
before counting the site-plan freedom it buys you.

**Do not go underground for land economics.** It costs two to three times what
it saves. Underground is justified only by aesthetics, topography, or a site so
constrained there is no alternative — all legitimate reasons for a $500 ADR
property, but they are design arguments, not land arguments.

Second-order effect worth modelling: a deck cuts the parking footprint out of
your impervious budget, which frees cover for **pool decks, terraces and hard
landscape** — the outdoor activation that is the product. On a 15%-cover site
that reallocation may matter more than the acreage.

this to City of Austin Development Services and Watershed Protection:

## The comparable that should change how you think about this

**Lakeway Resort & Spa sold in May 2026 for $37.8 million.** 168 rooms on an

with Sopris Capital — are repositioning it as "a boutique destination

That is roughly **$225,000 per key** for a waterfront resort with entitlements,

Three things follow.

1. **Your thesis is validated by capital that just moved.** Someone with

2. **It is also now a competitor**, and it will open before anything you start

**older, under-invested lodging and resort assets on good land** within the

# Operating playbook

Everything below is a thing to do, in order, with the actual steps.

## A. Getting listed inventory — two routes, run both

### Route 1 — a broker runs CoStar for you

CoStar is the institutional dataset; LoopNet is its public shop window. A broker
already pays for it, so this costs you nothing and returns better data than any
portal.

1. **Contact three firms.** Hospitality: **Marcus & Millichap Austin** (9600 N
   MoPac Expy — active hospitality division with Austin hotel transaction
   history), **JLL Hotels & Hospitality** Austin, **CBRE Hotels** Austin. Add
   one West Austin land/development specialist — AQUILA, Endeavor and Stream
   Realty all have Austin land practices.
2. **Send the broker brief** in the section above, verbatim. Do not soften the
   impervious-cover paragraph; it is what separates you from a tyre-kicker.
3. **Ask for one specific thing:** *"Can you run a CoStar search on these
   parameters and send me the export?"* Naming CoStar tells them you know what
   they have.
4. **Specify the export columns** — address, APN/parcel ID, acreage, zoning,
   existing building ft², year built, asking price, days on market, owner of
   record, listing broker. Ask for **CSV**, and send it to me: it drops straight
   into the tracker.
5. **Expect to sign a representation agreement.** Negotiate the term, the
   geography and the fee before signing, and keep the term short.
6. **Note on commissions:** the 2024 NAR settlement (guide §7) governs
   *residential* MLS practice. **Commercial brokerage was not covered**, and
   seller-paid buy-side fees remain normal in CRE. Ask directly how your broker
   is compensated on each deal — do not assume either model.

### Route 2 — you browse, I screen

Works today, needs no permissions, complements Route 1 rather than duplicating
it.

1. Create free accounts on **LoopNet** and **Crexi**.
2. Open the saved-search URLs generated by `build_search_urls.py --search
   austin-wellness-site`, adjust filters in the UI until the results look right,
   and **save each search**.
3. Turn on **email alerts** on every saved search. That is the intake channel.
4. When something looks plausible, send me the URL plus whatever the listing
   shows: **address, acreage, price, existing building ft², zoning**. A copy-paste
   of the listing detail is fine — I do not need it tidy.
5. I run it against the impervious-cover math, flag the deal-breakers, and file
   it in the tracker so price changes and days-on-market accumulate.

**Do not** install scrapers or automation against these sites. Their terms
prohibit it, and a scraper that silently breaks mid-search is worse than no
scraper — you would be deciding on a search that stopped working without saying
so.

## B. Finding the owner of an unlisted parcel — you can do this yourself

For any single parcel, this is a ten-minute job and needs no broker:

1. **Travis Central Appraisal District** (traviscad.org) — search by address or
   map. Gives the **owner of record**, mailing address, legal description,
   acreage, improvement ft², assessed land and improvement values, exemptions,
   and every taxing entity on the parcel.
2. **City of Austin property profile** — watershed and regulatory zone, zoning,
   city limits vs ETJ, impervious-cover limit, floodplain.
3. **Travis County Clerk** — recorded deeds, plats, easements, restrictive
   covenants. Confirms how long the owner has held it and what encumbers it.
4. **Write to the owner** at the TCAD mailing address. Short, specific, no
   pressure: what you are building, why this parcel, and that you are asking
   whether they would consider a sale. Entirely legal and routine — a large
   share of development land trades this way.

**The reason to automate it** is volume. Doing this for one parcel is easy;
doing it for the several hundred in West Austin that might qualify is not.
That is what the screener is for — it does the bulk pass, you do the deep dive
on the top ten or twenty it returns.

## C. Owner-side signals — how the ranking works

The screener flags owners for whom an approach is more likely to land. None of
these is evidence anyone wants to sell; they are a way to order your calls.

| Signal | What it reads | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| `held-NNyr` | Last recorded sale 20+ years ago | Long holds often sit with owners who have never been asked |
| `out-of-state:XX` | Owner mailing address outside Texas | Less attached to the asset, easier to reach, readier to trade |
| `ag-valued` | Agricultural valuation on close-in land | Usually a holding strategy rather than a farm — and it carries rollback exposure you must price |
| `improvement-worth-little` | Building value under ~35% of land value | You are buying the cover and the dirt; the structure is not the asset |

Signals **break ties, they never promote an unfit parcel**. A parcel that fails
the envelope test stays out no matter how motivated its owner might be.

| Verified live listings with price, acreage, status | LoopNet/Crexi/Realtor return 403 to automated access | A logged-in session, or a broker running the search |
| Per-parcel watershed and impervious cover | Requires the City of Austin property profile per parcel | Doable manually; ~15 min per candidate |
| Sold comparables | Texas is a non-disclosure state | An agent with MLS access, or CoStar |
| Off-market inventory | Not published anywhere | Brokers. At this size, two or three of them know every candidate |

The honest position: **at 5–20 acres, hotel-capable, West Austin, the qualifying
inventory is small enough that a good broker knows all of it.** The screening
work above is what makes you worth their time — but it does not substitute for
them.
