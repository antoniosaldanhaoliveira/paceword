These fit the strategy in the [site brief](austin-wellness-hotel-site-brief.md):

Run through the screener it passes comfortably — the only candidate that does.
And the physical form is unusually well suited: **thirteen buildings in a wooded

would be reorganising an existing campus rather than imposing a hotel on a pad.

**Two things to establish before anything else.**

**Jurisdiction.** Listings show this address as both *West Lake Hills* and

commercial use and a zoning fight with a council inclined to refuse. **Confirm
against the City of Austin jurisdiction map before spending another hour on it.**

**It is not for sale.** Leased and managed means an off-market approach: find
the ownership entity in TCAD, and go to them directly. That is the §3.6 play in
the guide, and a 1982 Class B office park in a softening Austin office market is
exactly the owner profile most likely to listen.

multiple closings, and a much longer clock.

## The structured-parking decision — resolved

I said this trade should be run before shortlisting. Here it is, at 145 spaces
(midpoint of the 120–170 the programme needs).

**2026 cost per stall:** surface $3,000–8,000 · above-grade deck $18,000–35,000 ·
underground $60,000–120,000.

| Option | Extra build cost | Land saved @15% (BSZ) | Value of land saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Above-grade deck** | $1.45M – $4.64M | 5.0 – 6.6 acres | **$2.49M – $8.28M** |
| Underground | $7.54M – $16.97M | 5.0 – 6.6 acres | $2.49M – $8.28M |

**Build the deck.** In the Barton Springs Zone an above-grade structure pays for
itself on land savings alone, and comfortably so at West Austin land values —
before counting the site-plan freedom it buys you.

**Do not go underground for land economics.** It costs two to three times what
it saves. Underground is justified only by aesthetics, topography, or a site so
constrained there is no alternative — all legitimate reasons for a $500 ADR
property, but they are design arguments, not land arguments.

Second-order effect worth modelling: a deck cuts the parking footprint out of
your impervious budget, which frees cover for **pool decks, terraces and hard
landscape** — the outdoor activation that is the product. On a 15%-cover site
that reallocation may matter more than the acreage.

## Next actions, in order

### A. The § 25-8-26 inquiry — do this first, it is free

The entire grandfathered-cover strategy rests on one unconfirmed point. Send
this to City of Austin Development Services and Watershed Protection:

> **Subject:** Redevelopment exception under § 25-8-26 — change of use to hotel
>
> I am evaluating existing commercial and office properties in the Barton
> Springs Zone for redevelopment as a hotel with associated spa and food and
> beverage.
>
> 1. Does the redevelopment exception under City Code § 25-8-26 apply where the
>    redevelopment involves a **change of use** — for example from office to
>    hotel — provided total impervious cover on the site is not increased?
> 2. If it does, what documentation establishes the site's existing impervious
>    cover for this purpose, and is a current survey sufficient?
> 3. Are there additional water-quality or treatment requirements triggered by
>    the change of use, even where cover does not increase?
> 4. Does the answer differ between the recharge zone, the Barton Creek
>    watershed, and the contributing zone?
>
> I am not asking for a determination on a specific parcel at this stage.

**If the answer is no, Priority 1 collapses and the search moves to the Lake
Austin watershed and Water Supply zones.** Ask before you spend anything else.

Do this before touring. It will remove candidates faster than any site visit.

# Operating playbook

Everything below is a thing to do, in order, with the actual steps.

## A. Getting listed inventory — two routes, run both

### Route 1 — a broker runs CoStar for you

CoStar is the institutional dataset; LoopNet is its public shop window. A broker
already pays for it, so this costs you nothing and returns better data than any
portal.

1. **Contact three firms.** Hospitality: **Marcus & Millichap Austin** (9600 N
   MoPac Expy — active hospitality division with Austin hotel transaction
   history), **JLL Hotels & Hospitality** Austin, **CBRE Hotels** Austin. Add
   one West Austin land/development specialist — AQUILA, Endeavor and Stream
   Realty all have Austin land practices.
2. **Send the broker brief** in the section above, verbatim. Do not soften the
   impervious-cover paragraph; it is what separates you from a tyre-kicker.
3. **Ask for one specific thing:** *"Can you run a CoStar search on these
   parameters and send me the export?"* Naming CoStar tells them you know what
   they have.
4. **Specify the export columns** — address, APN/parcel ID, acreage, zoning,
   existing building ft², year built, asking price, days on market, owner of
   record, listing broker. Ask for **CSV**, and send it to me: it drops straight
   into the tracker.
5. **Expect to sign a representation agreement.** Negotiate the term, the
   geography and the fee before signing, and keep the term short.
6. **Note on commissions:** the 2024 NAR settlement (guide §7) governs
   *residential* MLS practice. **Commercial brokerage was not covered**, and
   seller-paid buy-side fees remain normal in CRE. Ask directly how your broker
   is compensated on each deal — do not assume either model.

### Route 2 — you browse, I screen

Works today, needs no permissions, complements Route 1 rather than duplicating
it.

1. Create free accounts on **LoopNet** and **Crexi**.
2. Open the saved-search URLs generated by `build_search_urls.py --search
   austin-wellness-site`, adjust filters in the UI until the results look right,
   and **save each search**.
3. Turn on **email alerts** on every saved search. That is the intake channel.
4. When something looks plausible, send me the URL plus whatever the listing
   shows: **address, acreage, price, existing building ft², zoning**. A copy-paste
   of the listing detail is fine — I do not need it tidy.
5. I run it against the impervious-cover math, flag the deal-breakers, and file
   it in the tracker so price changes and days-on-market accumulate.

**Do not** install scrapers or automation against these sites. Their terms
prohibit it, and a scraper that silently breaks mid-search is worse than no
scraper — you would be deciding on a search that stopped working without saying
so.

## B. Finding the owner of an unlisted parcel — you can do this yourself

For any single parcel, this is a ten-minute job and needs no broker:

1. **Travis Central Appraisal District** (traviscad.org) — search by address or
   map. Gives the **owner of record**, mailing address, legal description,
   acreage, improvement ft², assessed land and improvement values, exemptions,
   and every taxing entity on the parcel.
2. **City of Austin property profile** — watershed and regulatory zone, zoning,
   city limits vs ETJ, impervious-cover limit, floodplain.
3. **Travis County Clerk** — recorded deeds, plats, easements, restrictive
   covenants. Confirms how long the owner has held it and what encumbers it.
4. **Write to the owner** at the TCAD mailing address. Short, specific, no
   pressure: what you are building, why this parcel, and that you are asking
   whether they would consider a sale. Entirely legal and routine — a large
   share of development land trades this way.

**The reason to automate it** is volume. Doing this for one parcel is easy;
doing it for the several hundred in West Austin that might qualify is not.
That is what the screener is for — it does the bulk pass, you do the deep dive
on the top ten or twenty it returns.

## C. Owner-side signals — how the ranking works

The screener flags owners for whom an approach is more likely to land. None of
these is evidence anyone wants to sell; they are a way to order your calls.

| Signal | What it reads | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| `held-NNyr` | Last recorded sale 20+ years ago | Long holds often sit with owners who have never been asked |
| `out-of-state:XX` | Owner mailing address outside Texas | Less attached to the asset, easier to reach, readier to trade |
| `ag-valued` | Agricultural valuation on close-in land | Usually a holding strategy rather than a farm — and it carries rollback exposure you must price |
| `improvement-worth-little` | Building value under ~35% of land value | You are buying the cover and the dirt; the structure is not the asset |

Signals **break ties, they never promote an unfit parcel**. A parcel that fails
the envelope test stays out no matter how motivated its owner might be.

| Blocked | Why | How to unblock |
|---|---|---|
| Verified live listings with price, acreage, status | LoopNet/Crexi/Realtor return 403 to automated access | A logged-in session, or a broker running the search |

The honest position: **at 5–20 acres, hotel-capable, West Austin, the qualifying
inventory is small enough that a good broker knows all of it.** The screening
work above is what makes you worth their time — but it does not substitute for
them.
